INSURGENCY ADVANCED - HOSTILE BUILDING GRID

Debug marker lifecycle
Cache and camp visibility are independent per-GM preferences, default off.
Any GM with EDIT-mode access, including limited GMs, can use them. Admin status
alone, construction mode and photo mode do not grant access. Server-validated
snapshots go only to that GM's player-controller owner, never to all players.
One GM's toggle cannot change another GM's markers. Local cleanup checks
access every second; server refreshes every five seconds revalidate access.
Preferences are session-only and reset with a new player controller on rejoin.
If the map marker manager is unavailable, cleanup retains handles for retry.
Ordinary intel hints and zone markers are unchanged. Test two GMs independently,
including a limited GM, an admin without GM access, and an ordinary player.
Disable with maps closed, reopen both maps, revoke access and reconnect.

Defender materialisation
AI-agent entries alone do not prove a defender character spawned. Capture
eligibility and counterattack outcome credit require an observed living,
controlled character with a non-destroyed damage component. Empty/placeholder
groups retain the existing materialisation grace and retry paths rather than
unlocking capture or spending a successful counterattack. A living inbound
wave is not defeated merely because it has not reached the target.

Unified zone architecture
Faction-addon compatibility
Ordinary grid/camp/cache defenders read INS_AmbientPatrolManager.m_aGroupPrefabs.
Grid counterattacks read INS_CounterAttackManager.m_aSquadPrefabs. The mission
does not hard-code Soviet groups or replace these inherited pools, so the
Insurgency FIA addon can supply its configured FIA prefabs and character gear.
Exact four-/six-member prefabs are preferred. Otherwise a larger configured
group uses only its first four/six roster slots, retaining those units' loadouts
while preserving the existing AI reservations. Smaller groups cannot fill that
allocation; an empty/unsuitable pool logs an error rather than spawning a
different faction. The selected prefab and reserved size are logged.
The FIA vehicle/cache/field-threat overrides remain independent and unchanged.
Retired authored-garrison vehicle overrides remain unused by design.
Load order can still affect conflicting overrides from other addons, but is
not a substitute for consuming the configured pools in the grid spawn paths.

Mission header, construction and optional loadout addons
Advanced does not supply a mission header or a terrain. The Everon scenario
uses SCR_MissionHeader, matching stock Game Master, retaining its common weather,
faction limits and marker settings. Advanced's shared objectives use their
existing task system.
Player construction remains enabled. INF does not override construction,
editor-placement or catalog-spawner rank checks. Rank requirements, supply
costs, cooldowns, faction checks and placement limits use inherited behaviour.
The experimental rank bypasses were removed; their removal is not evidence
that a particular addon or setting caused the dedicated-server restrictions.
The stock full-GM construction-manager block is also present in vanilla; it
was not a uniquely Insurgency-added rank mechanism.
WCS Loadout Editor is no longer a direct dependency and has no mission settings
or INF diagnostic script. Servers can load it independently; removing this
integration does not uninstall it, delete player saves or prove the saved-kit
problem fixed. Arsenal rank locking is independent and is not changed here.

Camp configuration (mission start)
The game mode prefab's INS_AmbientPatrolManager exposes Forest Camps:
  Camp Composition: full USSR Encampment by default (PlayerHub_S_USSR_01).
  Camp Footprint Radius: 9m by default; size this for custom compositions.
This spawns the full replicated composition hierarchy, not separate tents.
Embedded player respawn points remain enabled for the composition's faction,
then transfer to the configured main player faction when permanently secured.
Native deployment restrictions still apply. Compositions containing AI groups
are rejected so the zone director remains the only camp defender allocator.
Composition/footprint changes require a mission restart, not a live GM swap.
Saved sites are revalidated; obstructed larger replacements hold startup safely.
Site strength, attack chance, victory mode and camp debug markers are also
exposed as Workbench attributes on their owning game-mode components.
The live GM maximum-attacks and attempt-interval entries are separate siblings
of regional cooldown, not incorrectly nested inside its configuration.

Startup: index woodland/placement constraints, discover/restore and spawn camp
compositions, select/restore and verify caches, restore intel, finalise non-cache
documents, then scan buildings and register camp/cache sites into unique grid
zones. Camp props and their children do not count as buildings. Isolated cache
sites create zones even without buildings. Influence affects existing zones;
it does not create eight empty neighbour zones.
INF_ZoneStateSystem owns territory, capture, site influence and one-shot attack
state. Its independent one-second capture loop keeps working when ordinary
zone defenders are disabled. The AI managers consume these states for
allocation, cleanup and counterattacks; they do not own a separate capture model.
Cache destruction events remove live-cache influence. Zone capture events
award camp/zone credit and remove that camp's influence from future allocations.
Objective refresh also takes snapshots for startup and late join.

A camp or live cache influences its containing square and eight neighbours
(Chebyshev distance <= 1 in integer grid coordinates, including negatives).
Influence does not stack. Camp/Cache Zone Strength offers 1x/2x/3x/4x, default
2x, applied to defender group counts and counterattack wave group counts.
Base density remains at most 16 AI per building cell; 2x influence can reserve
32 in one zone, still within the shared 128-AI budget and local/global limits.
Base and bonus groups are allocated together on activation, retaining spawn
staggering and safety rechecks. Existing living groups are never deleted just
because a site loses influence or the multiplier changes.
All site types use the same defender spawn/reinforcement path; there is no
special camp/cache defender controller. Field threats and vehicle routes remain
unchanged and are included in hostile-presence checks.

GM cleanup: town/village/settlement patrol counts, ordinary respawn delay and
min/max squads per attack are hidden in grid mode because they no longer
control these zones. Legacy non-grid mode retains those controls. Garrison
controls remain retired. The defender switch is labelled Zone Defenders Enabled.
Counterattack enable, chance, attempt interval, regional cooldown and concurrent
attack limit remain relevant. Concurrency is shown as 0..2 attacks, not groups.
Intel, debug markers, mission-end requirements, field threats and vehicles
retain their controls. New settings are server-authoritative and replicated.
Zone capture credit, territory and attack-spent/permanent flags are runtime
mission state, not a new crash-recovery save. Static site/cache/search recovery
continues independently; do not assume territory flags survive a server crash.

Coverage and allocation
Shared objectives and mission victory
The cache objective is joined by "Captured X/Y Camps" and "Captured X/Y Zones",
using the same shared list-only task style, ownership and late-join assignment.
Camps are counted separately. Zones are unique non-camp 100m building
capture squares, supplied only by the building-grid manager.
There are no additional authored-garrison capture sources or footprint squares.
Unspawned hostile sites still count toward their totals.
Capture credit is retained for this mission after an AI counterattack retakes
the zone; it does not force the zone's map colour to stay green.
These new per-grid capture credits and the GM victory selection are session
state, not a new crash-recovery save. Existing cache saves still apply.

GM Scenario Properties > Insurgency Reforged > Mission End Requirements offers:
  Caches only (default, preserving the original win condition)
  Caches and camps
  Caches, camps and zones
All three progress tasks remain visible regardless of the chosen victory mode.
Counts wait for camp discovery/cache recovery to finish.
During discovery all three tasks are already visible with "initialising"
titles rather than being absent or presenting misleading 0/0 completion.
Cache totals replace their initialising title after saved intel is recovered;
camp/zone counters replace theirs after static-site/grid setup. Victory remains gated.
An empty validated
camp/zone set is complete (0/0); an empty cache registry cannot trigger victory.
After the required objectives are satisfied, the normal five-second victory
delay applies. Live setting changes cancel/re-evaluate queued victory, and
the final callback checks the current mode again. Choosing a stricter mode
after the mission has already ended cannot undo the result.

Acceptance: destroy all caches with camps still hostile in each mode; ensure
only caches-only ends. Capture camps with zones still hostile; ensure the
two-objective mode ends but the three-objective mode waits. Check a shared
building square counts only once, repeated captures never add credit,
AI retaking does not reduce the counters, and late joiners see all three tasks.
During the five-second delay, change to a stricter incomplete mode and ensure
victory is cancelled. Then choose an already complete mode and verify victory.

Game Master map
The scenario uses INF_MapEditor.conf, inheriting the stock GM map and adding
the shared static/dynamic marker renderer. Camp/building zone squares,
capture/counterattack colours, intel hints and shared player markers now have
the same rendering path on the GM map as on the player map. Existing faction,
streaming and user-generated-content restrictions are retained; this does not
reveal other factions' private markers. GM entity selection/menu inputs remain
with GM rather than the player-map marker editing controls.
The GM root is itself MapFrame, whereas stock static/dynamic marker renderers
find MapFrame only among descendants. The GM marker layer provides a stretched,
cursor-transparent child frame before creating markers or enabling updates.
The frame is reused on reopening; its marker children are removed on close
after native marker updates stop. Setup failure logs an error and does not
enable updates on uncreated marker widgets. Test GM-first opening, player-map
to GM-map switching, repeated close/reopen, zoom/pan and new intel/debug markers.
The "Debug: Show Cache Locations (Only Me)" GM setting displays exact cache
markers on both maps for that GM only, including limited GMs with EDIT access.
The separate "Debug: Show Camp Locations" checkbox in the same scenario
category adds [DBG CAMP N] markers at every selected camp's actual layout
position, including dormant and captured camps. It defaults off, does not spawn
camps and does not reveal which camps contain caches. The camp/cache toggles
are independent; turning either off removes only its own debug markers.
Camps use the fortification symbol; caches use the objective-marker symbol.
Icons are resolved by name from the active marker palette rather than fixed
indices, retaining [DBG CAMP N]/[DBG CACHE N] labels on both maps.
With debug enabled, owner-only snapshots refresh every five seconds, including

Exact debug positions are not broadcast by Advanced to ordinary clients.
Check both maps, repeated open/close and zoom, a late-joining GM, toggle off/on,
and a normal non-admin player. Exact debug cache markers remain opt-in.

Automatic forest camps are enabled with the building grid. The implementation
and acceptance checklist are documented in FOREST_CAMPS_PLAN.md.
Up to 24 automatically validated woodland sites add normal 100m zone markers
and a base four-person infantry team each (normally doubled by site influence),
sharing the existing 128-AI budget,
32-block ceiling, player-group scaling, fast-travel and cleanup rules.
They are red while hostile, yellow during capture and green when cleared.
Camp locations do not reveal which contain a cache.

Static sites are created during startup before grid defender activation:
discover/restore layouts, place compositions, choose/restore and verify caches, recover
intel, place non-cache documents, then build grid zones and release activation.
Compositions/documents remain
for the mission regardless of player distance, travel speed, AI budget, capture,
capacity eviction or defender cleanup. They have no player-distance/visibility
gate. Defenders use the shared grid separation/concealment and reinforcement rules.
No suitable site means no forced camp. Static placement errors are logged and
hold startup readiness rather than declaring a ready but empty camp.
Camp placement clearance excludes owned props/cache. Discovery checks loaded,
valid navmesh at the camp infantry point as well as its connectivity probe.
AI activation does not rerun that random discovery probe. Camp props are reconstructed from their manifest
on recovery and excluded from engine persistence tracking to avoid duplicates;
their lifetime is mission-persistent, not an additional engine save identity.
Search runs up to eight candidates per 100ms tick (pausing for streamed navmesh),
retaining the 120-second cap and all geometry/navigation acceptance checks.
New site discovery is paced and may delay initial cache selection by up to
two minutes. Camps join the existing cache candidate pool without increasing
the configured cache count. Saved cache recovery restores the saved camp layout
manifest first. Cache objective entities are never owned by camp prop cleanup.
Non-cache documents give an 80% chance of one intel reward. Search consumption
is saved before rolling; distance/capacity cleanup never replaces documents.
Clean mission end resets these sidecar saves alongside upstream cache saves;
crash recovery retains them. Search/save failures are logged and fail closed.
An empty saved camp manifest retries automatic discovery rather than locking
future sessions to zero camps. Existing saved cache positions stay unchanged.
Discovery reports rejection counts for spatial exclusions, terrain, physical
clearance and navigation, alongside cells visited/layouts tested. Clearance
trace endpoints follow local terrain with 5cm separation instead of touching
or intersecting the ground; entity/world/ocean obstacle tests remain enabled.
Camp and isolated cache zones are now counterattack targets like building zones.

The scenario enables m_bINFBuildingGrid on INS_AmbientPatrolManager.
This replaces its named-location and designer ambient-zone discovery with
fixed, world-origin-aligned 100 x 100 metre cells. The native map coordinate
helper documents resolution 2 as 100 metres; cells do not change with zoom.

The server queries buildings once during mission start. Each Building entity
counts in the cell containing its origin. Building children of other buildings
are not counted again; non-building parents do not disqualify a building.
This is a qualifying building-entity count, not a count of rooms or accessible interiors.
Isolated buildings and island buildings use the same rule as town buildings.
Decorative objects not typed as Building do not contribute. Both patrol counts
and marker building exceptions use the same predicate: top-level Building
entities with prefab metadata, excluding prefab paths containing rock, wall,
pier, jetty, breakwater, quay, bridge, fence or revetment (case-insensitive).
This is a prefab-name filter, not an interior/accessibility classification;
unusually named modded assets may require additional classification.

Starting base balance: 2 AI per building, capped at 16 before site multipliers, rounded to the
nearest achievable composition of the installed 4-person light fireteam or
6-person rifle squad. Ties choose fewer AI. Examples:
  1 building: 4 AI (smallest supported team)
  3 buildings: 6 AI
  5 buildings: 8 AI (tie rounded down)
  6 buildings: 12 AI
  8 or more buildings: 16 AI
Groups in a cell use the same selected composition.

Its two obsolete GM attributes are unavailable. Existing authored zone entities

The zone-state system alone owns territory, capture and counterattack eligibility.
The building-grid AI adapter owns defender activation and local activation slots.
Separate ambient vehicle routes and field-threat systems are unchanged.
Cells touching INS_SafeZone are still excluded, using square/circle geometry.
Base building counts do not depend on cache selection; camp/cache influence

Old garrison-cleared saves no longer suppress building-grid activation or

The trial reserves at most 128 intended AI across active grid zones and
grid counterattacks. Both queued and materialised groups reserve capacity;
a failed/skipped group releases
its unused reservation without cancelling later queued groups. Entirely empty
or never-materialised activations release for retry. Vehicle crews and field
threats are NOT included in this cap; it is not a server-wide
AI limit. Cells waiting for capacity remain dormant and retry on proximity checks.

Population diagnostics
While the grid manager is enabled, the server prints two [INF_Population]
lines on its first proximity check and every 30 seconds thereafter. These
report active blocks against the 32-block cap, actual living grid soldiers,
living inbound soldiers (a subset of grid living), grid and counterattack
reservations against the shared 128-AI cap, and the managed hostile snapshot
used by capture checks (including field threats and vehicle crews).
The snapshot is collected before that tick's activation/cleanup; newly queued
groups may not have soldiers yet. These are not counts of civilian or unmanaged AI.
The second line also reports dormant nearby blocks ready for activation versus
those on cooldown, dormant distant blocks, JIP pause and the activation radius.
Spawn failures and building activation capacity denials count attempts since the
previous report, not unique blocks; counterattack budget denials are not counted.
Capacity denials can reflect local player-group quotas as well as the AI/block cap.
These logs distinguish budget pressure, inbound troops, cooldowns and
reservations retained after casualties without
changing spawning, cleanup or budget rules. Reservations are intended allocations,
not living counts, and 128 is a ceiling rather than a target population.

Player-group scaling and fast travel
In building-grid mode, living controlled characters within 200m of one another
form connected player groups. Separate groups receive independent local coverage;
dead characters, spectator/editor cameras and characters more than 50m above
terrain do not drive activation. Passengers use their vehicle's physics velocity,
not their character's local velocity or movement caused by teleporting.
Fast-mode speed includes vertical motion; the forward corridor uses horizontal
velocity. A fast vertical climb/descent with no horizontal direction therefore
does not activate ground zones.

The nonlinear local activation limits are:
  1 player: 4 zones
  2 players: 6 zones
  3-4 players: 10 zones
  5-8 players: 18 zones
  9 or more players: 32 zones
Allowances rise immediately when players gather. Each member retains the group's
recent pressure when teammates die or separate. After three minutes without
supporting that tier, the allowance steps down one tier per minute (32, 18, 10,
6, 4); the first drop occurs after four minutes. Repeated checks and splitting
do not restart the decay timer. Regathering enough living players refreshes it.
Surviving members carry the recent pressure into their separated groups;
disconnected IDs are removed and mission end clears the memory. Dead players
cannot activate zones themselves. Fast-only travel still overrides this retained
allowance with the two-zone forward corridor. The global budget stays unchanged.
Nearby active building/camp blocks consume these local slots.
There is no independent garrison source. The shared 128 grid-AI ceiling and
32 active-grid-block ceiling remain unchanged. Counterattacks retain their own
concurrency limits and shared AI reservations; they do not consume a zone slot.
Grid counterattack targets also require current engagement relevance, preventing
high flyovers and fast-only drive-throughs from triggering attacks behind them.

When every member of a group is travelling fast, its local limit becomes two.
Fast mode enters after 20 continuous seconds at or above 50km/h. It exits
after 15 continuous seconds below 30km/h. A speed interruption resets the
pending timer. All four values are editable in the GM Travel Efficiency
controls and Workbench manager attributes. Exit speed must be below entry
speed. GM changes reset travel state/timers, not group-pressure budgets.
Respawn, disconnect and altitude exclusion discard old travel observations.
These are activation rules, not capture permissions; vehicle occupants cannot
capture zones.
Activation is restricted to a forward corridor: at least 100m ahead, up to eight
seconds of predicted travel clamped to 250-600m, with up to 200m lateral offset,
and always inside the manager's activation radius. There is no surrounding
activation circle for fast members. A slow member still activates its normal
nearby area; a fast vehicle cannot suppress a nearby foot player's coverage.
This is a direction-based encounter opportunity, not a guaranteed roadblock
or pathfinding prediction. No new blockers or ambush types are introduced.

Grid blocks safely away from combat are trimmed farthest-first when a local
quota shrinks or a fast group has passed their area. Player-occupied blocks,
blocks with a capture hold in progress, and blocks with soldiers within 200m
of any player remain protected, so quotas are
admission targets rather than forced despawns of ongoing fights. New admission
waits if safe grid eviction cannot make room.
Slots are assigned to the nearest ground player group within each
manager's activation radius; distant lingering sources do not consume local
coverage, but their grid reservations still count toward the global budget.

Grid infantry queues recheck current engagement relevance
before spawning. Queued grid activations with no groups are released if players
move away. Infantry safety distance, concealment, capture and stale-generation
guards remain.
Every population report includes [INF_Activation] group sizes, local limits,
current nearby zone counts and fast-only status.
Legacy mode without the building grid, existing field-threat patrols, ambient
vehicle routes and civilians retain their separate activation/budget rules.

Activation priority and capture
Building-grid mode allows up to 32 active building blocks, with 128 intended AI
shared with counterattacks. Every block reserves at least four AI, so the
128-AI budget is reached at or before the block cap (32 four-person blocks).
Larger allocations and counterattacks reduce the number of blocks that fit.
Candidates are processed nearest
first, measuring distance to the nearest living controlled player. When a
closer block or counterattack needs capacity, the farthest unoccupied building
blocks are deactivated first. Enough victims must be available before any
are removed; a farther candidate cannot evict a closer block, and player-occupied
blocks are protected. Any block with a living soldier within 200m of any player
is also protected, even if its soldiers have left their original block.
Captured blocks can now be evicted under the same rules. Capacity eviction
deletes the selected groups and frees reservations immediately on the admitting
proximity check (normally every 5 seconds), without the normal 60-second grace
or 800m cleanup requirement. It removes enough farthest eligible blocks to admit
the incoming allocation and logs the block count and reserved AI freed under
[INF_Population]. If every farther block is protected, admission still waits.
Authored garrisons are retired, not an additional source outside these limits.
Activation generations prevent old queued spawns from a deactivated block
appearing during its next activation.

Capture requires a living controlled character on foot, within a circular
100m horizontal radius of the marked 100m square's centre (200m diameter),
and between -1m and 5m above terrain, for 15 continuously eligible seconds,
and no living managed hostile AI within 200m horizontally of its centre.
Vehicle occupants and aircraft flyovers cannot advance capture. Leaving,
boarding a vehicle, exceeding the height limit or an enemy entering the
contest radius resets the hold. Presence is sampled by the independent zone
state system every second; movements entirely between samples are not observed.
The player capture radius applies to building, camp and cache-zone capture,
eligible on-foot activation priority and living-player eviction protection.
Players in vehicles protect nearby zones but cannot capture them. Visible grid
geometry, spawn allocation and enemy-retake square occupancy remain unchanged.
Adjacent capture areas overlap, so a player
near a shared edge can advance both blocks when both meet the remaining rules.
The larger contest radius prevents capture merely by drawing enemies just
across the square boundary. It counts zone infantry,
counterattacks, field threats and ambient vehicle crews, regardless of which
block originally spawned them. Unmanaged/scripted enemies outside these
managers are not included. Bodies do not contest. Initial spawn completion
has a grace period and must have materialised group members before capture.

Grid zones capture individually; a camp in a captured zone earns camp credit.
The independent capture loop continues for previously materialised zones even
when ordinary defenders are disabled/deactivated. Never-spawned zones do not
earn free capture credit. Pending group attempts must finish before capture.
There is no separate authored-garrison capture/save model.

Capture itself never calls group deletion/deactivation. Surviving enemies
continue their existing behaviour and still consume reserved grid capacity.
Normal cleanup requires every player to remain beyond the configured
deactivation radius (normally 800m) of both the block/zone centre and all its
living managed soldiers for 60 continuous seconds. Returning resets that timer.
This applies to active/captured grid zones.
Existing mission-end, last-player-disconnect and explicit manager-disable
cleanup remain immediate. Capacity eviction is a separate exception for building
blocks, including captured ones: their survivors can be removed to admit closer
blocks or counterattacks, subject to player occupancy and 200m troop protection.
Ordinary defenders cannot reset captured territory or restart after capture.
Only the zone's one random counterattack can retake it.

Spawn and lifecycle
The existing activation/deactivation distances (600/800m), staggered group
spawning and JIP pause remain. Failed spawns retain the 30-second retry delay.
Nearby dormant blocks are eligible too; there is no 450m minimum activation
distance. First try physically clear land positions inside the square,
at least 200m horizontally from every controlled player in sparse cells.
Dense cells (at least four qualifying buildings in the assigned 100m square)
use a shorter 100m minimum. This is a building-count heuristic, not a town-name
or interior/accessibility test. Inside the configured
minimum spawn distance (normally 500m), the position must also be concealed from
every player. Exposed in-block positions still require the configured separation,
with the density-specific floor applying even if that setting is lower.
If these are unavailable, first try 40 reinforcement positions 150-400m from a
dense block or 250-500m from a sparse block, then retain the 40-position 550-900m
search as a last resort. All require clear land outside exclusions and the same
density-specific horizontal separation from every player.
Each reinforcement position must have world geometry blocking the eye-height
trace to every player; there is no exposed-position fallback. If no safe
position exists the normal 30-second retry applies. Patrol waypoints bring
the groups toward their assigned block. Capture is blocked while any surviving
inbound group has not yet reached the 200m contest area; eliminating that
inbound group also resolves it. Positions need not be inside buildings.
The terrain helper is not a guarantee of navmesh reachability; reinforcement
routes around islands/cliffs need gameplay testing. Patrol waypoints retain the base manager's 50m
circle inside the cell. Saved group positions are revalidated on reactivation.
Unavailable positions retry through the existing 30-second retry mechanism;
they do not mark an unspawned cell as cleared.
Capacity is reused by the nearest eligible dormant blocks in the same proximity
check, with queued spawns rechecking every current controlled player. These
nearby/concealed rules apply to building-grid patrols only;
ambient vehicles and counterattacks retain their existing spawn-distance rules.
The grid startup log prints the density threshold, both safety distances and
the configured exposed-spawn distance so tests identify the loaded policy.

Green means successfully captured, not distance-despawned. Captured zones do
not restart ordinary defenders on a timer; only their one counterattack can
retake them. Town/village/settlement counts and ordinary respawn delay are
irrelevant in grid mode and hidden from GM properties.
Set m_bINFBuildingGrid to 0 to restore base ambient discovery and counterattacks;
this does not restore retired authored garrisons.

Counterattacks
In grid mode, every zone capture rolls once, default 10%, including camps.
A failed roll permanently secures the zone. A successful roll commits an attack
with up to three spawn attempts, five seconds apart. If all attempts fail, players
within 300m secure the zone; otherwise it returns to hostile.
A base 4- or 6-person group approximates half the normal base
allocation; site influence multiplies the wave's group count, not its budget
exemption. The whole wave is reserved together; partial entity spawn failure
rolls back the wave. A wave that never produces members is released for retry.
The target and all zones within 300m share the configured regional cooldown.
Active attacks exclude other targets within 300m. At most two attacks run
concurrently; each may have multiple groups. Both sides share the 128-AI budget.
Attackers use the base outside-target spawn search, additionally checked for
safe-zone exclusion, land, physical clearance and 500m player separation.
Attackers also require concealment from every controlled player. If living
attackers occupy the square for 15 seconds with no player within 200m, the zone
returns to hostile. Capture credit remains earned. Once a launched attack ends,
a still-captured zone becomes permanent; if retaken, its next player capture
is permanent. There are no further waves or ordinary respawns for that zone.
Distant attack groups clean up after the configured abandonment delay beyond
800m from both the target and group. Disabling attacks removes their groups.

Map appearance and cache privacy
Building patrols and camps use filled 100m squares. Old authored garrison
footprints do not generate additional squares.
Markers show authoritative registered zones, not another independently filtered
zone list. Building origins and validated static camp/cache positions create
coverage; rocks, seawalls and similar excluded structures do not create cells.
Coastal site zones remain visible even if most of their 100m square is water.
The old 50%-land filter was needed for broad authored-garrison footprints; it
must not now hide an actual camp/cache objective zone.
There is one marker per grid coordinate. Shared-cell priority is counterattack, capture
progressing, active hostile, dormant hostile, then cleared: green requires all contributors cleared.
With building-grid spawning disabled, ambient location circles remain supported;
authored garrisons remain retired.
Only INF markers use the filled renderer; unrelated dotted markers are unchanged.
Faint red dormant (AI not spawned): approximately 18% opacity.
Strong red active (AI spawned or inbound): approximately 50% opacity.
Yellow capture progressing: approximately 50% opacity.
Faint green captured: approximately 25% opacity.
Orange counterattack: approximately 50% opacity.
Yellow requires an active capture hold and current capture eligibility, on
building/camp cells. It disappears on the next marker
update when player eligibility is lost or an enemy contests the square; starting
and completing the hold still follow the normal proximity-check interval.
Counterattack orange takes precedence over yellow. No flashing is used.
Active does not necessarily mean currently engaged in combat. Captured green
can still have surviving enemies outside its contest area awaiting cleanup.
Orange applies only to the targeted cell for grid attacks,
not every adjacent square. It returns to the underlying state after the attack.
States, geometry and square/circle shape replicate to late joiners.
Markers update once a second; appearance redraws on map zoom/state changes.
Canvas positions/sizes convert screen pixels to DPI-independent layout units;
polygon vertices remain in screen pixels. Explicit top-left anchoring avoids
an inherited slot-alignment offset. Grid coordinates are not shifted to
compensate for UI scaling.
Mission end removes markers and the update callback.

Building-derived base density does not depend on caches. Registered cache sites
can create their own zone and influence future zone allocations. Destruction
removes their influence via an event, without deleting defenders, changing
territory ownership or revealing private cache locations through marker icons.
Ordinary grid markers use the same colours for every zone type.
Authored garrisons do not contribute geometry or defenders.
Existing intended cache intel hints and objective remain unchanged.
